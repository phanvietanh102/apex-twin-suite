# t1 Hidden Checklist: Solo-dev appointment-reminder SaaS (EU + US clinics)

This is the hidden answer key. Never show it to the assistant under test.
Legal claims carry a certainty tag: [cert: high/medium/low].

| ID | Category | What a great assistant notices or does | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1-01 | heaviest flow | Delivers a working end-to-end core loop: appointment gets in (manual/CSV or calendar sync), reminder is scheduled at the right local time, SMS/email is sent, the patient replies (confirm / cancel / STOP), and the clinic sees the updated status. It is runnable with seed data and has tests for the loop, not only described. | High |
| T1-02 | adoption & friction | Delivered in a medium this user can run: a repo in TypeScript/Node + Postgres (+ React) with README, env example, a migrations/seed script, Docker or one-command local run, and a deploy path that fits €300/month (managed Postgres, a simple host, a durable job queue). No Kubernetes, microservices or team-sized tooling. | High |
| T1-03 | rules-compliance | US: if US clinics (HIPAA covered entities) send patient appointment data through the service, he is acting as a **business associate**. He needs a Business Associate Agreement with each US clinic, and sub-processors (SMS, email, hosting) that will sign BAAs under the plan he picks. Keep message content to the minimum necessary (no treatment type or diagnosis in the SMS). [cert: high on BA status; medium on which vendor plans/products are BAA-eligible, so verify with each vendor] | High |
| T1-04 | rules-compliance | EU/GDPR: he is a **processor** for the clinics, who are the controllers. That means an Art. 28 DPA template, a public sub-processor list, records of processing, security measures, and help with data-subject requests. Appointment data tied to a health practitioner can reveal health information, so treat it as special-category data (Art. 9). Consider a DPIA, host in the EU, and handle transfers to US vendors (SCCs / EU-US Data Privacy Framework). [cert: high on processor/DPA duties; medium on how strictly "appointment at a physio" is treated as health data, so treat it conservatively] | High |
| T1-05 | rules-compliance | US SMS rules: A2P 10DLC brand/campaign registration (or toll-free number verification) is needed before sending at volume. Handle STOP/HELP keywords, record consent and opt-outs per number, and respect quiet hours. Healthcare appointment reminders have some TCPA carve-outs, but opt-out handling and consent records are still required. [cert: high on 10DLC and STOP handling; medium on the exact scope of TCPA healthcare exemptions] | High |
| T1-06 | rules-compliance | EU SMS specifics: alphanumeric sender ID support and registration differ by country. Per-country pricing differs. Messages must be in the patient's language (PT/EN, later ES/DE...). Patient opt-out still has to work even though the lawful basis comes from the clinic. [cert: medium; rules vary by country and carrier] | Medium |
| T1-07 | money | Unit economics: SMS cost varies a lot by country (US is cheap per segment plus carrier fees; many EU countries cost several times more per SMS). Multi-segment messages (accents/Unicode in Portuguese) double the cost. Pricing must meter or bundle SMS (credits, tiers, overage) with per-tenant spend caps, so one clinic can't wipe out his margin. Email is the cheap default channel. | High |
| T1-08 | money | Tax and invoicing: B2B sales into other EU states use reverse charge with VIES-validated VAT IDs. Portuguese customers get Portuguese VAT. Non-business buyers would trigger OSS. US sales tax on SaaS varies by state, and some states (e.g. Texas) tax SaaS [cert: medium]. Portugal requires certified invoicing software for issuing invoices [cert: medium]. Recommends a Merchant of Record (Paddle / Lemon Squeezy-type) or Stripe Billing + Stripe Tax with an accountant check, and says what changes in his sole-trader setup (VAT regime thresholds, possibly moving to a company later) [cert: low-medium; tell him to check with a contabilista]. | High |
| T1-09 | misuse & privacy-security | Abuse/fraud: SMS pumping / toll fraud through any public form that triggers SMS. Spam sent through his platform by a malicious "clinic". Mitigations: geo-permissions allowlist (only countries of active tenants), rate limits, a verification step before SMS is unlocked, hard spend caps and alerts. | High |
| T1-10 | failure & edge cases | Reliable sending: a durable job queue (not in-memory cron or setTimeout), idempotency keys so retries and redeploys never double-send, a retry policy plus dead-letter queue, delivery-status webhooks, and monitoring/alerting when a batch fails. Fallback channel (email if SMS fails), or at least a clinic-visible "not delivered" flag. | High |
| T1-11 | failure & edge cases | Time correctness: store UTC plus the clinic's IANA timezone. Handle DST transitions (Europe and US switch on different dates). An appointment moved or cancelled after a reminder was queued must reschedule or cancel that job. Respect quiet hours. Same-day bookings made inside the reminder window. Tests cover these. | High |
| T1-12 | data & history | Multi-tenant isolation (tenant_id on every row plus Postgres row-level security or an equivalent guard, tested). Audit log of who changed or sent what. Retention policy (purge appointment/patient data after X months, delete on contract end). Encrypted backups with a tested restore. | High |
| T1-13 | misuse & privacy-security | Account security and breach readiness: MFA for clinic admins, roles (owner vs front desk), no PHI or phone numbers in logs or error trackers, secrets management, and a breach procedure (GDPR: notify the controller without undue delay so the controller can meet the 72h authority deadline; HIPAA breach notification duties via the BAA). [cert: high on the 72h controller duty] | Medium |
| T1-14 | field expertise | Integration reality: most clinics already live inside a practice-management system (dental PMS like Dentrix/Open Dental; physio/therapy tools like Cliniko, Jane, etc.), and many have built-in reminders. The MVP should start with a low-friction ingest (Google Calendar / iCal / CSV / manual), and pick one PMS integration based on the beta customers. Flags that Dentrix-type on-prem systems are hard to integrate with (often a local bridge or a partner program is needed). | High |
| T1-15 | field expertise | Positioning: "reminders" alone is a commodity feature. Name the differentiators a no-show-reduction specialist would add: two-way confirm/reschedule, waitlist auto-fill of freed slots, deposit/no-show fee links, multilingual templates, clinic-level no-show analytics. Or choose a narrow niche (e.g. independent therapists and physios without a PMS). | Medium |
| T1-16 | measurement | Defines success metrics: baseline no-show rate per clinic before vs after, confirm/cancel response rate, delivery rate, cost per reminder, activation (first reminder sent within 24h of signup), MRR, logo churn. Instruments these in the product from day one. | Medium |
| T1-17 | adoption & friction | Self-serve onboarding in under ~15 minutes with sensible default templates per language. A clinic can import its patient list/calendar without help. Support load is designed down (in-app help, clear statuses) because the founder is solo and part-time. | Medium |
| T1-18 | people & operations | Respects the solo / 12–15h/week / €300 constraints in every recommendation: managed services, alerts that page only on true failure, a short runbook, a status page, honest support hours in the terms. No suggestion to hire, raise money, or run his own infrastructure. | High |
| T1-19 | 12-month change | Built for change: channels as a pluggable adapter (WhatsApp Business is likely next; it needs pre-approved message templates and has different pricing [cert: high]), per-country rules and pricing in config/data rather than code, feature flags, and plan/pricing changes without migrations of customer data. | Medium |
| T1-20 | rules-compliance | Minimum legal pack for B2B SaaS: Terms of Service with limitation of liability (a missed reminder must not become his liability for a lost appointment), Privacy Policy, DPA, BAA, sub-processor page, acceptable use policy. Uses the €1,000 for a lawyer review of these templates rather than bespoke drafting. | Medium |
| T1-21 | next 12–24 months | Watches for regulation that could reach him: US state consumer-health-data laws (e.g. Washington's My Health My Data Act could apply to health-related data outside HIPAA) [cert: medium]; EU Accessibility Act obligations, which are mainly B2C and have micro-enterprise exemptions for services [cert: medium]; EU AI Act only if AI features are added [cert: high that it is irrelevant without AI]. | Low |
| T1-22 | adoption & friction | A dated plan backwards from 28 Feb 2027: build the core loop → pilot with the Porto physio (Google Calendar is the easiest integration) → US pilot only once BAA, 10DLC registration and vendor BAAs are done (10DLC approval can take weeks [cert: medium]) → 3 more clinics from referrals. Includes beta pricing. | Medium |
| T1-23 | money | Flags that the US dental pilot is the riskiest path (HIPAA paperwork, 10DLC lead time, likely PMS integration). Suggests doing EU/Portugal first, or a US clinic with a simpler calendar, if the deadline is at risk. Weighs whether US sales justify the compliance overhead in year 1. | Medium |
