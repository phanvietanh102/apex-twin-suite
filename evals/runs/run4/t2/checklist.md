# t2 Hidden Checklist: Small English law firm, matters / key dates / limitation / client comms

This is the hidden answer key. Never show it to the assistant under test.
Legal claims carry a certainty tag: [cert: high/medium/low]. This list is not legal advice. Where a rule is described rather than cited, that is deliberate.

| ID | Category | What a great assistant notices or does | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| T2-01 | heaviest flow | The heaviest workflow works end to end: enquiry → conflict check → AML/ID check (for conveyancing/probate) → engagement / client care letter → matter opened with responsible fee earner and key dates → automatic reminders with escalation → updates and correspondence logged → file closure with a retention date. Each step names who does it (Sue / Priya / solicitor). | High |
| T2-02 | adoption & friction | Delivered in a medium this non-technical firm can run and maintain, on £150/month and £2,000. Either (a) built on their existing Microsoft 365 (SharePoint Lists / Outlook shared calendar / Power Automate reminders / Forms for intake), or (b) a recommendation of an established small-firm practice management system (Clio, LEAP, Actionstep or similar) with a clear build-vs-buy comparison. No bespoke code they cannot maintain. If it builds, the steps must be doable by the IT man or by Priya. | High |
| T2-03 | field expertise | Limitation date logic per matter type, with a "basis / trigger date / calculated by / checked by" field: PI 3 years from the cause of action or date of knowledge; contract and most tort 6 years; latent damage negligence 3 years from knowledge with a 15-year longstop; for a minor, time runs from the 18th birthday; time doesn't run while the claimant lacks capacity. Employment tribunal claims (e.g. unfair dismissal) are typically 3 months less one day, and ACAS early conciliation pauses/extends the clock. Notes that the calculation is the solicitor's judgement and the system only records and alerts. [cert: high on headline periods; medium on edge rules, so tell them to confirm per matter] | High |
| T2-04 | failure & edge cases | Fixes the root cause of the near miss, a single point of failure: every limitation/critical date is entered by one person and independently verified by a second (four-eyes), and a backup diary is kept (system plus a second calendar or printed weekly key-dates report). Margaret's paper diary becomes the *secondary* record, not the only one. | High |
| T2-05 | people & operations | Escalation and cover: reminders at staged intervals (e.g. 3 months / 1 month / 2 weeks / 1 week / 2 days before), acknowledgement required, automatic escalation to the other solicitor if not acknowledged or if the fee earner is absent. A sabbatical handover: bulk reassign Margaret's matters to David before 1 March, with a handover review of every open matter's dates. | High |
| T2-06 | field expertise | Conveyancing key dates: exchange and completion; SDLT return and payment within 14 days of completion [cert: high]; Land Registry application lodged within the priority period of the official search (30 working days) [cert: high]; mortgage offer expiry; search/indemnity validity; undertakings given on completion. | High |
| T2-07 | field expertise | Probate key dates: IHT due by the end of the sixth month after the month of death or interest runs [cert: high]; IHT account normally within 12 months of the end of the month of death [cert: high]; claims under the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975 normally within 6 months of the grant, so hold back distribution [cert: high]; statutory notices to creditors (Trustee Act 1925 s.27, at least 2 months) [cert: medium-high]. | Medium |
| T2-08 | rules-compliance | AML: conveyancing and probate/trust work fall within the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 regime [cert: high]. Needed: customer due diligence/ID verification and a matter risk assessment at intake, source of funds/wealth checks for purchases, a firm-wide risk assessment, and keeping CDD records at least 5 years after the business relationship ends [cert: high]. The intake flow must block opening a regulated matter until CDD is recorded. | High |
| T2-09 | rules-compliance | Conflict check at intake against all parties (client, other side, related persons, property address), across the 9-year Excel history, with the outcome and checker recorded. The SRA Code prohibits acting where there is a conflict or significant risk of one, with limited exceptions [cert: high]. | High |
| T2-10 | rules-compliance | Client care / transparency: client care letter template with costs information and complaints procedure. SRA Transparency Rules require published price and service information for some services, including residential conveyancing, uncontested probate and certain employment tribunal claims [cert: high]. Complaints: the firm must handle complaints first, and the Legal Ombudsman can normally step in after 8 weeks; time limits for going to LeO must be signposted [cert: high]. Built as standard templates in the system. | Medium |
| T2-11 | money | Recognises that client money lives under the SRA Accounts Rules and belongs in the existing accounts package. Does not build ledgers or balances in the new system; links to them at most (e.g. a "funds received / cleared" status on a conveyancing matter). Respects "don't touch accounts". | Medium |
| T2-12 | misuse & privacy-security | Payment-diversion ("Friday afternoon") fraud controls: firm bank details given at engagement in writing and by phone; every client-facing template states "we will never change our bank details by email"; callback verification on a known number before any outbound payment; MFA on all M365 accounts; email authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) checked by the IT man; consider a secure portal or encrypted email for documents. | High |
| T2-13 | misuse & privacy-security | UK GDPR: ICO data protection fee paid [cert: high]; privacy notice; special category data (health records in PI, possibly criminal data) handled with restricted access; DSARs answered within one month [cert: high]; breach reporting to the ICO within 72 hours where required [cert: high]; a retention schedule. Typical practice is around 6–7 years after closure for most files, longer for wills/deeds and for minors' claims, but there is no single statutory period [cert: medium], so it should be a firm decision recorded in the system. | High |
| T2-14 | data & history | Migration without losing history: import the 9-year Excel matter list (deduplicate and normalise client names for conflict searching). Transcribe every future date from the paper diaries for all ~140 open matters. Each open matter's key dates are reconciled and signed off by the fee earner. Run old and new in parallel for a defined period. | High |
| T2-15 | field expertise | Undertakings register: undertakings (especially in conveyancing) are recorded, assigned, and tracked to discharge. Failing to honour an undertaking is a serious regulatory matter [cert: high]. | Medium |
| T2-16 | data & history | Wills/deeds storage register (originals held in the safe, with location and withdrawal log) and a file closure checklist that sets a destruction review date. Probate and wills firms are often asked years later "do you hold Mum's will?" | Medium |
| T2-17 | rules-compliance | Documents a written key-dates and risk procedure (owner, review cadence, near-miss log) that can be shown to insurers at PII renewal and fits the firm's compliance roles (COLP oversight). [cert: medium that insurers specifically ask; high that a documented process helps] | Medium |
| T2-18 | heaviest flow | Client communications: incoming calls/post logged against a matter reference by Sue (works from her iPhone), emails filed to the matter (Outlook-to-matter folder/SharePoint, or the PMS's Outlook add-in), standard update letters, and "no contact for X weeks" chasers so clients don't feel ignored (a big complaint driver in conveyancing). | Medium |
| T2-19 | adoption & friction | Designed for the least technical user (Margaret): a printable/emailed weekly "my key dates" report she can read on paper, minimal clicks, a one-page guide, a short training session for each person, the new paralegal onboarded into the new system from 11 January rather than Excel. Explicitly plans for Margaret's adoption, since she named it as a risk. | High |
| T2-20 | measurement | Measures whether it works: % of open matters with verified key dates (target 100% before 1 March), overdue/unacknowledged reminders per week, time from enquiry to engagement letter, weekly 10-minute key-dates review in a partners' meeting. | Medium |
| T2-21 | 12-month change | Built to absorb change without a developer: adding a matter type or a date rule, new and leaving staff (offboarding: remove access and reassign matters), template edits. No hard-coded rules or personal mailboxes as the system of record. | Medium |
| T2-22 | next 12–24 months | Next steps to raise: Cyber Essentials certification (often requested by insurers/lenders) [cert: medium]; digital ID verification for conveyancing clients [cert: medium]; keeping an eye on SRA consumer-protection reforms around client money and firm risk [cert: low-medium]. | Low |
| T2-23 | people & operations | Respects constraints and the deadline with a phased plan: key dates and limitation register live and verified first (before Christmas or by mid-January), intake/conflict/AML next, comms and templates last, and full handover readiness tested in February before 1 March 2027. Keeps Outlook, the accounts package and the shared-drive folders unchanged, as asked. | High |
