## The three realistic routes

| Route | What it looks like | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Off-the-shelf legal practice management software** (for example LEAP, Clio, Quill, Osprey Approach, Actionstep. Check current UK pricing; it's usually charged per user per month) | Matters, key dates, tasks, time recording, client comms, documents, and often SRA-compliant legal accounting, all in one place | Built for law firms. Supported and backed up for you. Insurers and the SRA recognise these systems. Outlook integration usually included | Monthly cost for 4 users. Some setup and data migration. You work their way |
| **B. Build on Microsoft 365, which you probably already pay for** (SharePoint Lists or Microsoft Lists for matters and key dates, a shared Outlook calendar, Power Automate reminders, Teams/SharePoint for files) | A structured matter register with automatic reminder emails, replacing the big spreadsheet | Little or no extra cost. Familiar tools. I can design and help configure most of it | Someone in the firm has to own and maintain it. No legal accounting. Reminders depend on automations you have to look after |
| **C. Custom-built app** | Bespoke database and web app | Exactly what you want | For a 4-person firm I wouldn't recommend it. Hosting, security, backups, GDPR and ongoing maintenance all become your problem, and it's the riskiest home for limitation dates |

**My provisional view:** if you handle client money, or you're doing billing and accounts separately, **Route A** usually wins, because one system replaces the spreadsheet, the diaries *and* the accounts headache. If you don't hold client money and the budget is tight, **Route B** is a solid, inexpensive option, and I can help you build it properly. Either way, I'd steer you away from C.

Whichever route you pick, the key-dates part needs these features to actually be safe:

- **Every matter has a limitation date recorded, or is explicitly marked "N/A" with a reason.** No blanks.
- **Two independent reminders** (for example the system plus a second person's calendar), with escalating warnings at about 3 months, 1 month, 2 weeks and 1 week.
- **A named fee earner and a named back-up** on each matter, so dates are covered during holidays and sickness.
- **A weekly key-dates review**: a 10-minute look at everything due in the next 8 weeks.
- **Dates are worked out and checked by a solicitor.** The system reminds; it doesn't decide the limitation period.
- **An audit trail** of who changed a date and when.
- **Security fit for client data:** multi-factor authentication, UK/EU data hosting, backups, and access removed when people leave. This matters for UK GDPR and for your PII insurer, and Cyber Essentials is worth considering.
